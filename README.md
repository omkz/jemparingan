# JEMPARINGAN

A Unity archery project inspired by Javanese jemparingan, currently developed for desktop/Web and eventual Meta Quest experimentation.

## Open and play

- Unity version: **6000.2.14f1** (`ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt`).
- Open `Assets/Jemparingan/Scenes/JemparinganPlayableEnvironment_v05.unity`.
- Enter Play Mode and choose **Traditional (1)** or **Modern (2)**. Press **M** to return to the mode selector before the first shot or after the round.
- On desktop: **hold left mouse** to draw, **release** to shoot, **hold right mouse + move mouse** to aim, and **R** to restart.
- The Joglo, rice-field landscape and target scene are already committed. Do not regenerate the scene merely to change gameplay scripts.

## Active modes

- **Traditional** (primary): 12 arrows across **three levels (4 arrows each)**: stationary bandulan, gentle pendulum swing, then a wider but playtested-conservative swing (default 0.16 m at 1.12 rad/s), with slightly varied (deterministic) rhythm. The target and its existing suspension cord move together, with continuous speed changes and no sudden teleport between stages. Zone scoring stays **molo (red) = 3**, **jangga (yellow) = 2**, **awak (white) = 1**. This is a Mataram-inspired adaptation, not a claim of complete historical authenticity. On desktop, Traditional uses the same Modern first-person bow/hand rig, sight, draw animation, recoil, shot audio, arrow cam and compact HUD; only target appearance, scoring and target motion differ. Quest presentation is left unchanged.
- **Modern** (retained): ring-scored targets, physical sight, optional arrow camera and a three-stage progression with a moving target.

### Shared bow and hand visual polish
The procedural bow and desktop left-hand meshes are generated by the existing Unity C# components (no Blender imports or duplicate prefabs). The curved limbs use a slightly smoother, gently recurved profile; the same string-tip and grip anchors are preserved. Leather grip stitching is now one pooled-at-build mesh instead of 14 separate decorations. The shared desktop hand has rounder fingers, knuckles and thumb, with the existing grip animation. Matte wood/leather and restrained brass finish use the existing URP Lit materials.

In Unity, use **Tools > Jemparingan > Run Gameplay QA (Traditional + Modern)**, then play **both modes** and inspect idle, partial/full draw, sight alignment, grip contact, release recoil, zoom and arrow flight. Check both hands and string attachment remain aligned. This pass does **not** replace or validate the Meta Quest hand-tracking visual rig.

### First-person hands-only visual polish v2

**No full-body character and no second/right desktop hand.** The shared desktop left-grip viewmodel has warmer, matte skin, a smoother palm/first-knuckle silhouette, and a subtly rolled indigo cloth cuff which hides the animated sleeve-to-wrist connection. The existing **13-ring / 12-side** animated sleeve gets a small fabric taper and folds **without additional renderers, mesh rebuilds, or topology changes per frame**. Finger contact endpoints, grip pressure pivots, bow/string/nock anchors, input, hitboxes, gameplay and Quest-specific hand rig are unchanged.

**Test after pulling:** open the committed `JemparinganPlayableEnvironment_v05.unity` and **press Play** (no Environment Apply menu). In **Traditional and Modern**, inspect the wrist at rest/partial/full draw, turning aim, grip pressure, release recoil, and arrow cam. Check that no cuff ring floats above the skin, no sleeve end appears in the camera, no hand clips through the leather grip, and the target/HUD remain clear. Confirm gameplay QA and compare Profiler before/after if targeting lower-spec hardware. Source-only checks are not Unity compilation, visual QA, or Quest device verification.

### Target object polish v1 — Traditional and Modern

This pass is **visual-only** and keeps the existing game rules. The single new `JemparinganTargetTrim` helper produces small combined meshes at runtime while the target is built. **Traditional:** fine dark-thread binding bands and two muted ivory grain accents on the existing white/yellow/red bandulan, without moving the zone boundaries or pendulum cord. **Modern:** a low-poly wood edge and eight subdued brass fixing pins outside the 0.50m scoring face, plus two wooden stand braces combined into one separate mesh. The impact/pulse animation stays on the reactive face; the stand remains under the moving root.

The polish adds **one MeshRenderer for Traditional**, and **two MeshRenderers per Modern target** (three targets, seven total). Renderers reuse existing shared materials, without new shaders, colliders, physics bodies, lights, input changes, or per-frame mesh generation. Owned meshes are destroyed with their GameObjects. Small visual details may need different contrast depending on target distance, lighting and build resolution; **Unity visual QA and FPS impact remain unverified**.

**Try it:** open `Assets/Jemparingan/Scenes/JemparinganPlayableEnvironment_v05.unity` and press **Play**; no Apply menu and no scene regeneration. In Traditional, review the narrow red Molo/yellow Jangga/white Awak silhouette while still and swaying. Check the cord joins the tip, the bands remain attached through recoil, and hits award 3/2/1. In Modern, check all three target numbers, target readability at each stage and moving middle board, ring scoring 10/8/6/4/2 and impact reaction. Compare Profiler render passes/frame time against baseline; avoid treating source checks as a device benchmark.

### Shared procedural arrow visuals

The nocked preview and pooled flying arrows now reuse one procedural C# mesh: a warm tapered bamboo shaft, shallow bamboo nodes, a beveled dark steel point, three swept deep red feathers, brown thread bindings and a compact split ivory nock. Existing LightWood, BowIvory, BowLeather and Red materials are reused; ArrowSteel is the only added palette tone. No Blender assets or textures are required. The mesh has 656 triangles, one renderer and five material slots per arrow, is built once per game and is released with its visual owner.

Launch origin, aim, projectile motion, swept-tip detection, scoring, pooling and trails are unchanged. The flying mesh's point aligns with the existing local Z = 0.36 m swept tip; the nocked root still follows the existing string animation. See [arrow visual validation](docs/validation/arrow-visual-polish/README.md) for Unity preview images and testing scope. After Unity imports the scripts, start a fresh Play Mode session to rebuild the procedural art. In both modes, inspect idle/half/full draw, release, arrow cam, hits, misses and restart; confirm the red feathers and steel point stay consistent and the nock remains seated on the string.

**Classic and Challenge have been removed** from the runtime mode selector, scoring and mission logic. The old Challenge medal system and Classic arcade bonuses are no longer part of active gameplay. Existing historical documentation and screenshots may still describe earlier builds.

The scenic-only preview lives in `Assets/Jemparingan/Scenes/JemparinganEnvironmentPreview_v01.unity`; the older indoor scene `JemparinganOptimized_v02.unity` is retained as a historical/legacy scene. The playable outdoor scene is the recommended one.

### Joglo + afternoon lighting polish v1 (Unity Editor only)

This pass improves the **existing playable Joglo v5 scene without regenerating or overwriting it**. In Edit Mode, open `Assets/Jemparingan/Scenes/JemparinganPlayableEnvironment_v05.unity`, then choose **Tools > Jemparingan > Environment > Apply Joglo + Lighting Polish v1**. Review in Scene/Game view, and save the modified scene with **Ctrl+S** only when satisfied. For a code-only PR checkout, no scene file changes until you explicitly use that Editor menu.

The tool applies versioned URP Lit materials to the joglo only (warmer teak, quieter trim, clay roof, stone), adjusts the golden-hour sky/ambient and the existing directional sun while keeping realtime shadows **off**, and adds a single static combined 144-triangle mesh of eight wooden knee braces and four narrow fascia lines. It is idempotent: running it again does not create another detail group. It does **not** touch the bandulan, archery lane, target, cameras, input, gameplay or projectile paths. The old material assets and saved scene are not overwritten. The **Create Playable Environment v5** generator remains a separate, destructive/regeneration workflow and should not be used just for this polish.

**After Apply**, use **Tools > Jemparingan > Environment > Save Joglo Polish to Scene v1** to explicitly save the open playable v5 scene and verify the new joinery object and skybox reference on disk. The save command is intentionally separate from Apply so the visual changes can be reviewed first. The command reports the absolute file path and fails clearly if the scene cannot be persisted. Then run **Tools > Jemparingan > Environment > Validate Joglo Polish v1**. It checks 144 joined triangles, one detail renderer, no new colliders/shadows, **multiple actual polished joglo materials**, the exact committed skybox asset, and whether the joinery group is present in the **saved .unity scene file**, not only the open Editor hierarchy. The report includes the absolute saved scene path. Earlier builds checked outdated material display names and incorrectly rejected a correctly assigned skybox named `GoldenHourSky_JogloPolish_v01`; use the corrected validator on this branch. In Play Mode, inspect whether the roof silhouette remains legible, the aiming lane stays unobstructed, both modes still shoot properly and the scenery is not blown out. Measure rendering in Unity Profiler; the combined mesh is cheap but overall Quest FPS is still unverified. **If you commit/push the modified scene, also commit the newly generated materials, mesh, skybox and their `.meta` files** under `Assets/Jemparingan/EnvironmentPreview/`; those assets are produced by the Editor tool, not bundled in this code-only PR.

### Landscape Polish v1 — automatically applied in Play Mode

The v5 playable scene and scenic preview receive a lightweight, **visual-only** landscape pass automatically when loaded in Play Mode. **No Environment > Apply menu or scene regeneration is needed.** The standalone `JemparinganLandscapePolish.cs` adds static cross-blade rice clumps over existing paddy plots (12 per plot), small tropical-tree and bamboo-leaf silhouettes, and subtle broken facets along the distant mountain ridge. It reuses the materials already referenced by the scene; no Blender model, texture, new shader, realtime lighting, physics collider, or per-frame vegetation animation is introduced.

The additions are combined by material into a small number of static renderers, and their generated mesh allocations are cleaned up on scene unload. All rice stays outside the central 5m corridor. The original scene file, target anchors, bandulan, input, scoring, bow/arrow mechanics, trees and existing mountain meshes are unchanged.

**Try it:** checkout the new branch, open `JemparinganPlayableEnvironment_v05.unity`, press **Play**, and view the paddies / distant ridges from the seated shooter. Check Traditional and Modern, target visibility at 10/20/30m, and the frame cost in **Window > Analysis > Profiler**. An optional **Play Mode** read-only check is at `Tools > Jemparingan > Environment > Validate Landscape Polish v1`; it checks expected mesh/renderers, no added colliders/lights/cameras, and that rice stays out of the aiming lane. **This is not a real Unity compile or GPU benchmark; actual Editor / Quest testing remains required.**

Unlike the earlier **Joglo + Lighting Polish v1** action, this step is a runtime visual overlay: you will see it in Game/Play view without modifying or committing the saved scene. To preserve the earlier joglo material assignment for another clone, you still need to commit the modified `.unity` scene after applying and saving that earlier editor pass; committing generated `.mat` files alone does not modify scene references.

## Validation

In Unity Edit Mode, use **Tools > Jemparingan > Run Target Hitbox Checks**, then **Tools > Jemparingan > Run Gameplay QA (Traditional + Modern)**. In Play Mode, check Traditional sight alignment and the no-snap miss case, shared bow/hand pose and recoil, minimal unobstructed HUD, Traditional hit zones, stage boundaries at shots 4 and 8, smooth pendulum turns with an attached cord, sparks appearing at the precise hit point and differing for Molo/Jangga/Awak, subtle score underline pulse, restart resetting to a still bandulan, 12-shot results showing Molo/Jangga/Awak totals, Modern stages, arrow camera, input and replay. These checks supplement—not replace—real Unity compilation and headset testing.

## Historical development notes (v0.9 and earlier)

- A 12-shot round with three difficulty stages, combo scoring, bullseye/stability bonus, and local best-score persistence.
- Reusable arrow pool, swept arrow-tip collision, modest aim assist, and trajectory preview.
- Procedural pendopo, bow, and bandulan, with simple synthesized audio.
- **v0.3:** bandulan recoil and a reusable eight-spark impact burst; arrows briefly stick to the bandulan before returning to the existing pool. No imported VFX package required.
- **v0.4:** pendopo backdrop, drapes and lanterns, supporting frame/plaque behind bandulan, small bow ornaments, phase label for three stages, 12-shot progress pips, and combo callout.
- **v0.5:** minimum sustained draw before firing, stable RASA hint refresh, clearer ready/release/miss feedback, different hit/bullseye/miss colors, and a smaller target collision zone aligned to the visible bandulan.
- **v0.6:** in-world introduction (dismissed by first draw), brief stage transition banner, end-of-round results (score, accuracy, bullseye, highest combo, RASA bonus), correct new-record detection, and replay via R (Editor) or release-and-hold string gesture (~0.7s).
- **v0.7:** six-step RASA charge indicators beside bow, smoothed aim/hand-position stability with tremor tolerance and short release grace, ready chime, and optional predicted target position (small green dot) in stages II/III. The Quest hand adapter now uses the bow-string hand axis while both are pinched, but **has not been hardware tested**.
- **v0.8:** original low-volume procedural wind ambience, different wooden impact sounds for hit vs bullseye, gentle stage-change cue, brief floating score feedback, short pooled arrow trails, and end-of-round titles based on accuracy and RASA composure. No third-party art/audio asset or external SDK required.
- **v0.9:** safeguards against double releases when restarting with the string held; arrow pool capacity scales to configured shots; exact trajectory preview now matches arrow gravity integration; deterministic score/stage/accuracy helpers and a **21-check Unity Editor gameplay QA** menu.
- Supplemental imported OBJ/MTL models in `Assets/Models/`; the demo builds its own simple geometry at runtime.
